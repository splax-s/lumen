Feel it first. Name it second.

Every lesson takes about five minutes. You play with the idea before it gets a name, so the word lands on something you already understand.

Meet Lumi. It asks before it tells.

Lumi watches what you do in the simulation and helps the way a good teacher would: a nudge, then a clue, then a demo. Never the answer straight away.

NudgeYour creatures keep starving. What could you change?
ClueLook at the speed slider. Fast creatures burn food quickly.
DemoWatch what happens when I slow them down a little.

Your progress is a place.

Your town starts as a pencil sketch. Each lesson you finish paints a real object in its real colours. The kettle steams, the swing moves, the sunflower turns gold.

One lesson, three depths

Play · about 6 to 10

Frozen water holds hands in rings, with gaps in the middle. Same pieces, more space.

Explain · about 11 to 15

Ice is less dense than water: the same stuff spread over more space, so it floats.

Deep · 16 and up

Hydrogen bonding forms a hexagonal lattice. Ice is about 0.92 g/cm³, water 1.00 g/cm³.
